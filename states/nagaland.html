Nagaland

The Land of Festivals • State of Rich Naga Heritage

About Nagaland

In the northeastern realm of India, Nagaland is like a dream come true, where time seems to stand still, and every step unveils a treasure trove of culture and nature. This enchanting land, known as the "Land of Festivals," offers a glimpse into the vibrant and rich Naga heritage, breathtaking landscapes, exquisite cuisine, vibrant festivals, and unique shopping experiences.

On your poetic journey through Nagaland, explore these facets and uncover the hidden gems that make this land an irresistible destination. From misty mountains and ancient tribal villages like Khonoma to historical monuments in Dimapur and Kohima, Nagaland preserves a pristine cultural identity.

Top Attractions in Nagaland

Naga Heritage Village (Kisama)

The cultural epicentre showcasing traditional morungs of all major Naga tribes, famous for hosting the annual Hornbill Festival.

📍 Open Live Directions

Khonoma Village

India’s first green village, renowned for terrace cultivation, brilliant bio-reserve conservation, and ancient Angami warrior history.

📍 Open Live Directions

Kohima War Cemetery

A serene and poignant WWII memorial on the slopes of Garrison Hill carrying historic epitaphs honoring fallen soldiers.

📍 Open Live Directions

The Kachari Rajbari Ruins, Dimapur

Mysterious 10th-century monolithic mushroom-domed pillars reflecting the heritage of the Dimasa Kachari kingdom.

📍 Open Live Directions
HERITAGE & CELEBRATIONS

Culture, Art & Festivals

🎭 Tribal Heritage & Crafts

Home to diverse indigenous Naga tribes with striking traditional shawls, intricate cane-bamboo handicrafts, and distinctive warrior heritage.

🍛 Traditional Naga Cuisine

A unique and flavorful culinary tradition featuring smoked meats, fermented bamboo shoots (*Axone*), fiery Raja Mircha chilies, and organic herbs.

🎉 Major Festivals

Celebrated as the Land of Festivals, highlighted internationally by the renowned **Hornbill Festival** held every December, alongside Yemshe and Tokhü Emong.
